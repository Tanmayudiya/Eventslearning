## 5. Guided Learning

Guided Learning focuses on understanding a subject through explanations, questions, examples, and practice.

Students can use it to:

- Learn concepts from the basics.
- Break difficult topics into manageable steps.
- Practice with questions.
- Identify areas that need revision.
- Develop understanding instead of memorizing answers.

**Example prompt:**

Teach me binary search from the basics. Use a simple array, explain every step in easy English, and give me a practice question at the end.

**Key takeaway:** AI can support learning more effectively when we use it to understand and practice, not just obtain final answers.

## 8. Gemini Notebook: Organizing Learning Resources

Notebook was one of the particularly useful features discussed during the session.

It provides a dedicated space to work with supported learning resources, which may include documents, PDFs, website links, YouTube videos, and other materials.

- Educational video links.
- Official documentation.
- Relevant website links.
- Code repositories.

They can then ask questions, request summaries, and compare explanations using the available notebook capabilities.

**Important:** Source-based prompting does not guarantee perfect accuracy. Always check answers against the original material.

**Key takeaway:** Organizing learning resources in one place can make studying more focused and structured.

## 10. Prompt Engineering: The PARTS Framework

Prompt engineering is the practice of writing clear instructions so that an AI system better understands the task, context, and expected output.

During the session, we discussed the **PARTS framework** for structuring prompts.

- **P — Persona:** Define the role you want the AI to play.
- **A — Aim:** Clearly state what you want to achieve.
- **R — Recipients:** Identify who the response is intended for.
- **T — Theme:** Specify the topic, tone, or style.
- **S — Structure:** Define how the output should be organized.

### Practical example

Instead of writing:

"Explain Artificial Intelligence."

Use a structured prompt:

**Persona:** Act as an experienced AI/ML tutor.

**Aim:** Explain Artificial Intelligence from the basics.

**Recipients:** College students who are new to AI.

**Theme:** Simple, engaging, and educational.

**Structure:** Provide a definition, practical examples, key applications, and a short summary.

This prompt gives the AI clearer instructions about the role, task, audience, style, and format.

**Key takeaway:** A well-structured prompt helps communicate what you actually need and can improve the relevance of the response.

## 11. Iterative Prompting

Prompting is an iterative process. This means that we can improve an output through repeated instructions, feedback, and refinement.

For example, if an AI-generated study guide is too technical, we can ask it to:

1. Simplify the explanations.
2. Add relatable examples.
3. Highlight important definitions.
4. Include practice questions.
5. Organize the final material into clear sections.

Instead of expecting perfection from the first response, we improve the result step by step.

**Key takeaway:** Better outputs often come from reviewing, refining, and communicating our requirements clearly.

## 12. Human in the Loop

Another important principle discussed during the session was keeping a human in the loop.

This means that humans remain involved in reviewing, guiding, testing, and evaluating AI-generated outputs.

AI can make mistakes, produce unsupported claims, or misunderstand instructions. Human judgment is therefore essential.

For example, when using AI to generate code, we should understand the logic, run the program, test different inputs, and verify the result before relying on it.

The same principle applies to assignments, research reports, images, and presentations.

**Key takeaway:** AI can assist our work, but we remain responsible for evaluating and using its output appropriately.

## 14. AI-Assisted Image Verification

We discussed using AI to examine images and investigate whether they might have been generated or edited using AI.

A useful prompt could be:

"Analyze this image for possible signs of AI generation or editing. Explain your observations, distinguish evidence from speculation, and suggest ways to verify the image's origin."

AI may identify visual clues, but it cannot always determine which specific chatbot or model created an image.

For stronger verification, look for original files, provenance metadata, creation records, or reliable information from the original publisher.

## 15. A Practical AI Workflow for Students

The features explored during the event can support different stages of a student's learning process.

**Step 1 — Collect resources:** Gather notes, PDFs, presentations, and relevant links.

**Step 2 — Organize with Notebook:** Group supported sources around a particular subject.

**Step 3 — Understand with Guided Learning:** Explore difficult concepts through examples and practice.

**Step 4 — Research with Deep Research:** Investigate topics that require broader context.

**Step 5 — Develop content with Canvas:** Organize ideas into structured notes or documents.

**Step 6 — Create visuals:** Explore educational illustrations and presentation graphics.

**Step 7 — Review everything:** Verify important claims, check the original sources, and ensure that you understand the final output.

This workflow shows how AI can support learning from collecting information to creating and reviewing study material.

The goal is not to automate learning completely. It is to make learning more organized, interactive, and effective.
